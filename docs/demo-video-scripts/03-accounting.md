# Demo Scripts — Accounting Manager

**Role:** Level 4. Full control over financial records: chart of accounts, journals,
periods, bank accounts and reconciliation, expenses, payments, and all financial
reports. The system auto-posts most transactions to the general ledger, so much of
this role is reviewing and reconciling rather than keying entries.

**Total videos in this role:** 9

---

### Section 1 — Accounting dashboard tour
**Estimated length:** ~1:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Log in as Accounting Manager; land on the accounting dashboard.
2. Pan across the menu: Accounts, Journal Entry, Periods, Bank Accounts, Expenses,
   POS Remittances, Purchase Payments, Reports, Posting Status.

**Narration:**
> Welcome to SweetTooth ERP training. In this video, we'll tour the Accounting
> module.
>
> When you log in as the Accounting Manager, your dashboard gives you a snapshot of
> the branch's finances. From the menu you can reach the chart of accounts, manual
> journal entries, accounting periods, bank accounts and reconciliation, expenses,
> point-of-sale remittances, supplier payments, the financial reports, and the
> posting status monitor.
>
> One thing to understand up front: SweetTooth posts most transactions to the general
> ledger automatically. When a sale is made, stock moves, or payroll is run, the
> accounting entry is created for you. So a large part of your job here is reviewing,
> reconciling, and reporting — not typing entries by hand.

### Section 2 — Chart of accounts
**Estimated length:** ~2:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Accounting → Accounts**. Show the chart of accounts grouped by type
   (Assets, Liabilities, Equity, Income, Expenses).
2. Open an account; show its number and details.
3. Create a new account; assign its type and number.
4. Edit an account name.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll look at the chart of accounts — the backbone of your
> accounting.
>
> Open Accounts. Every account is grouped by type: assets, liabilities, equity,
> income, and expenses. Each one has a number and a name. The numbering follows a
> standard order, so assets come first, then liabilities, and so on.
>
> To add an account — say, a new expense account for cleaning — click add, choose the
> account type, give it a number in the right range, and name it. To rename an
> existing account to match the business's own terminology, just open it and edit.
>
> Because the system posts automatically, it's important that each department has its
> revenue and cost accounts mapped correctly. If a posting ever fails, a missing or
> wrong account is usually the cause.

### Section 3 — Manual journal entries
**Estimated length:** ~2:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Accounting → Journal Entry**.
2. Create a new entry; add debit lines and credit lines; confirm it balances.
3. Add a description and date. Post the entry.
4. Show the posted entry in the general ledger view.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll record a manual journal entry. You'll use this for things the
> system doesn't post automatically — for example, opening balances, corrections, or
> accruals.
>
> Open Journal Entry and start a new one. Add your lines: every entry must balance, so
> total debits have to equal total credits. The screen shows you the running total, so
> you'll know when it's balanced.
>
> Give the entry a clear description and the correct date, then post it. Once posted,
> it flows straight into the general ledger, where you can find it alongside every
> other transaction.

### Section 4 — Accounting periods
**Estimated length:** ~2:00
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Accounting → Periods**. Show existing periods and their status.
2. Create a new period for the current month; set it Open.
3. Close an old period.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll manage accounting periods.
>
> Open Periods. A period is usually a calendar month, and it has a status — open or
> closed. Transactions can only post into an open period, so you need a period open
> for the month you're working in.
>
> Create the current month and set it to open. It's good practice to create the next
> couple of months in advance so they're ready. When a month is finished and
> reconciled, close it. Closing a period locks it, which stops anyone posting late or
> backdated entries and protects your reported figures.

### Section 5 — Bank accounts and reconciliation
**Estimated length:** ~3:00
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Accounting → Bank Accounts**. Show the list with balances.
2. Add a bank account: name, number, type, opening balance.
3. Go to **Bank Reconciliation**. Match system transactions against a statement.
4. Mark items as reconciled; show the reconciled balance.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll set up a bank account and reconcile it.
>
> Open Bank Accounts. Add each real business account with its name, account number,
> type — savings or current — and its opening balance as of your start date.
>
> Reconciliation is how you confirm the system agrees with the bank. Open Bank
> Reconciliation and bring in your bank statement. The screen lists the transactions
> the system has recorded. Match each one against the statement, ticking them off as
> they agree. When everything is matched, the reconciled balance should equal your
> closing statement balance. Any difference points you to a missing or duplicated
> transaction to investigate.

### Section 6 — Expenses and quick expense
**Estimated length:** ~2:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Accounting → Quick Expense**. Record a simple expense (amount, account,
   payment source). Save.
2. Show **Expense Entries** list.
3. Briefly show **Expense Imports** for bulk entry.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll record expenses.
>
> For a one-off cost, use Quick Expense. Enter the amount, choose the expense account
> it belongs to, and select where it was paid from — cash or a bank account. Save it,
> and the system posts it to the general ledger automatically.
>
> All your expenses appear in the Expense Entries list, where you can review them. If
> you have many expenses to enter at once — for example from a spreadsheet — use
> Expense Imports to bring them in together rather than one by one.

### Section 7 — POS remittances and purchase payments
**Estimated length:** ~3:00
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Accounting → POS Remittances**. Show daily till takings awaiting
   remittance. Confirm/record a remittance to the bank.
2. Go to **Purchase Payments**. Show outstanding supplier balances (accounts payable).
3. Record a payment against a supplier invoice.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll handle money coming in from sales and going out to suppliers.
>
> First, point-of-sale remittances. At the end of each day, the cash taken at the
> tills needs to be remitted — moved from the till into the bank. Open POS
> Remittances, and you'll see the takings waiting to be remitted. Record the
> remittance, and the system moves the amount from the till clearing account into the
> bank, keeping your cash position accurate.
>
> Now, purchase payments. When the inventory team buys on credit, the amount sits as
> a balance owed to the supplier — your accounts payable. Open Purchase Payments to
> see who you owe. To settle an invoice, record a payment against it, choosing the
> bank account you paid from. The supplier's balance reduces, and the payment posts to
> the ledger.

### Section 8 — Posting status monitor
**Estimated length:** ~2:00
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Accounting → Posting Status**.
2. Show successful postings and any failures.
3. Open a failed posting; show the reason.
4. Retry a failed posting.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll use the posting status monitor — your safety net for automatic
> postings.
>
> Open Posting Status. Because the system posts sales, stock movements, payroll, and
> more on its own, this screen lets you confirm everything landed in the ledger. Green
> means posted successfully.
>
> If a posting fails, it shows up here with the reason — most often a missing account
> mapping for a department. Open the failure to see the detail. Once you've fixed the
> underlying cause, you can retry the posting directly from here, and it will go
> through. Checking this screen regularly keeps your books complete.

---

### Section 9 — Financial reports and sending to the MD
**Estimated length:** ~3:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Accounting → Financial Reports**.
2. Open Trial Balance, then General Ledger, then Income Statement, Balance Sheet,
   Cash Flow.
3. Show Budget vs Actual and AP Aging.
4. Export a report to PDF.
5. Compile a report and use **Send to MD**.

**Narration:**
> In this final accounting video, we'll run the financial reports.
>
> Open Financial Reports. Start with the Trial Balance — it lists every account with
> its balance and confirms the books are in balance. The General Ledger gives you the
> full detail behind any account.
>
> Then you have the three core statements. The Income Statement shows profit and loss
> for a period. The Balance Sheet shows the financial position — assets, liabilities,
> and equity — at a point in time. And the Cash Flow Statement shows how cash moved.
>
> There are management reports too: Budget versus Actual compares performance against
> plan, and the Accounts Payable Aging shows how long supplier balances have been
> outstanding.
>
> Any report can be exported to a PDF. And when you want the Managing Director to see
> a report, compile it and use "Send to Managing Director" — it appears on their
> reports dashboard. That completes the Accounting videos.

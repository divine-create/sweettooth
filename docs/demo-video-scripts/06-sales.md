# Demo Scripts — Sales (Manager & Staff)

**Role:** Sales Manager (Level 3) oversees the sales floor and POS; Sales Staff
(Level 1) run the daily shift. These videos follow the enforced daily workflow, so
they suit both — sections 2 to 5 and 8 are the core staff workflow; sections 6, 7,
and 9 are manager/supervisor-focused.

> **Note for recording:** The system enforces the order **Stock Opening → POS →
> Shift Closing**. You cannot open the POS until stock opening is done, so record the
> sections in order. Sales screens are department-scoped to your station.

**Total videos in this role:** 9

---

### Section 1 — Sales dashboard tour
**Estimated length:** ~1:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Log in as Sales; show the sales dashboard.
2. Pan across the menu: Stock Opening, POS, My Sales, Dispatches, Production
   Requests, Expiry Alerts, Stock Monitor, Callbacks, Variance Resolution, Shift
   Closing, Analytics.

**Narration:**
> Welcome to SweetTooth ERP training. In this video, we'll tour the Sales dashboard,
> which is where the point of sale lives.
>
> When you log in for sales, your dashboard is built around the daily shift. From the
> menu you can reach stock opening, the point of sale, your own sales, dispatches from
> the kitchen, production requests, expiry alerts, the stock monitor, callbacks,
> variance resolution, and shift closing.
>
> The most important thing to understand is the order of the day. The system makes you
> follow a sequence: first you open stock, then you sell at the point of sale, and at
> the end you close your shift. You can't skip ahead — the point of sale stays locked
> until stock opening is done. Let's walk through the day in order.

### Section 2 — Clocking in and selecting a shift
**Estimated length:** ~1:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. From login, go to the shift selection screen.
2. Clock in; select the shift type.
3. Land on the sales dashboard with an active shift.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll start the day by clocking in.
>
> Before you can do anything on the sales floor, you need an active shift. After
> logging in, you're taken to the shift screen. Clock in, and select your shift type —
> for example morning or afternoon. This records your attendance and opens up the work
> screens for the rest of the day. Without this step, the system won't let you reach
> stock opening or the point of sale.

### Section 3 — Stock opening
**Estimated length:** ~2:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Sales Dashboard → Stock Opening**.
2. Show the list of products at the station.
3. Enter the opening quantity counted for each. Submit.
4. Show that the POS is now unlocked.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll do stock opening — the first task of every shift.
>
> Open Stock Opening. You'll see the products available at your station. Before you
> sell anything, you count what's physically there and enter the opening quantity for
> each one. This is your starting position for the shift, and it's what the system
> uses later to work out how much you sold and whether anything is unaccounted for.
>
> When you've entered everything, submit it. Notice that the point of sale is now
> unlocked. Until stock opening is submitted, the system keeps the till closed, so
> there's always an accurate starting count behind every shift.

### Section 4 — Using the point of sale
**Estimated length:** ~4:00
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Sales Dashboard → POS**.
2. Add products to a sale by tapping them; adjust quantities.
3. Apply a discount (note: may require manager approval).
4. Choose a payment method (cash, card, transfer); enter amount tendered.
5. Complete the sale; show / print the receipt.
6. Mention automatic GL posting and stock reduction.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll process a sale at the point of sale — the heart of the day.
>
> Open the point of sale. To build a sale, tap the products the customer is buying.
> Each tap adds it to the order, and you can adjust quantities as you go. The running
> total updates on screen.
>
> If the customer gets a discount, apply it here. Depending on the setting, a discount
> may need a manager or supervisor to approve it before it goes through.
>
> When the order is complete, choose how the customer is paying — cash, card, or bank
> transfer. For cash, enter the amount given and the system shows the change. Complete
> the sale, and a receipt is produced for the customer.
>
> Behind the scenes, two things happen automatically. The products you sold are
> deducted from your station's stock, and the sale posts to the general ledger as
> revenue. You just sell — the system keeps stock and accounts in step.

### Section 5 — Requesting more stock from the kitchen
**Estimated length:** ~2:00
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Sales Dashboard → Production Requests**.
2. Create a request; add products and quantities needed. Submit.
3. Show the request status.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll ask the kitchen for more stock.
>
> When a product runs low at your station, you don't walk to the kitchen — you send a
> production request. Open Production Requests, create a new one, and add the products
> and quantities you need. Submit it, and it goes straight to the right kitchen.
>
> You can track the status here. When the kitchen fulfils it, the stock is dispatched
> to your station, which we'll see in the next video. This keeps the sales floor
> stocked without anyone leaving their post.

### Section 6 — Receiving dispatches
**Estimated length:** ~2:00
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Sales Dashboard → Dispatches**.
2. Show incoming dispatches from production.
3. Confirm / receive a dispatch; show stock added to the station.
4. Briefly show **Stock Monitor** and **Expiry Alerts**.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll receive stock sent from the kitchen.
>
> Open Dispatches. When production fulfils your request, the stock arrives here as a
> dispatch. Check that what was sent matches what you asked for, then confirm receipt.
> The quantities are added to your station's stock and become available to sell.
>
> Two related screens help you stay on top of stock during the shift. The Stock
> Monitor shows your live quantities at a glance, and Expiry Alerts warns you about
> anything nearing its expiry date, so you sell it first and avoid waste.

### Section 7 — Variance resolution *(supervisor / manager)*
**Estimated length:** ~2:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Sales Dashboard → Variance Resolution** (supervisor+).
2. Show a variance between dispatched/opening stock and recorded sales.
3. Investigate and resolve the variance with a reason.

**Narration:**
> In this video, for supervisors and managers, we'll resolve a variance.
>
> A variance is a difference the system spots between what should be there and what
> actually is — for example, opening stock plus dispatches, compared with sales and
> closing count. When the numbers don't line up, it appears here for review.
>
> Open Variance Resolution and look at the flagged item. Investigate the cause — it
> might be a miscount, breakage, or an unrecorded sale — and resolve it with a reason.
> Only supervisors and above can do this, because resolving a variance affects the
> records. Recording the reason keeps the floor accountable and the stock honest.

### Section 8 — Closing the shift
**Estimated length:** ~2:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Sales Dashboard → Shift Closing**.
2. Enter the closing stock count for each product.
3. Show the sales summary and cash to remit.
4. Submit to close the shift.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll close the shift — the last task of the day.
>
> Open Shift Closing. Count what's left at your station and enter the closing quantity
> for each product. The system compares your opening stock, the dispatches you
> received, and your sales, against this closing count, and shows you the summary —
> including the cash you should be remitting.
>
> Review it, make sure the cash matches, and submit. That closes your shift cleanly.
> The day's sales flow through to accounting, and the cash you remit is picked up in
> the point-of-sale remittances we covered in the accounting videos.

# Demo Scripts — Inventory Manager

**Role:** Level 3. Full control over stock and purchasing: items, categories,
suppliers, purchases with batch and expiry tracking, stocks and stock takes,
material request approvals, and inventory reports. Read-only on production; no
access to sales or accounting operations.

> **Note for recording:** Inventory operations require an active shift. Clock in and
> select a shift before recording the operational sections.

**Total videos in this role:** 8

---

### Section 1 — Inventory dashboard tour
**Estimated length:** ~1:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Log in as Inventory Manager; clock in / select shift; land on the inventory dashboard.
2. Pan across the menu: Items, Item Categories, Suppliers, Purchases, Stocks, Stock
   Takes, Material Requests, Health Checks, Shift Closing, Analytics.

**Narration:**
> Welcome to SweetTooth ERP training. In this video, we'll tour the Inventory module.
>
> First, note that inventory screens need an active shift, so I've clocked in and
> selected my shift. Now the dashboard gives me an overview of stock — items low on
> stock, items expiring soon, and recent movements.
>
> From the menu you can reach items and their categories, suppliers, purchases, stock
> levels and stock takes, material requests from production, health checks, shift
> closing, and analytics. We'll walk through each one.
>
> One key idea before we start: in SweetTooth, an "item" is something you hold in
> stock — a raw material or a resale good. That's different from a "product", which
> is something the kitchen makes or the shop sells. Inventory manages items.

### Section 2 — Items and categories
**Estimated length:** ~3:00
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Inventory → Items**. Show the list with search and filters.
2. Open an item: show name, category, unit of measure, unit cost, sell price,
   reorder level.
3. Create a new item with all fields. Save.
4. Set a reorder level and (where relevant) a sell price.
5. Show **Item Categories** briefly.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll manage items.
>
> Open Items. This is your full catalogue of everything held in stock. You can search
> by name and filter by category. Open an item to see its details: the category it
> belongs to, its unit of measure — for example grams, litres, or pieces — its unit
> cost, and where relevant a sell price.
>
> To add an item, click add and fill in those same fields. The unit cost is what you
> pay to buy it; getting it right matters because it drives both inventory value and
> recipe costs in production.
>
> Two fields are easy to forget but important. The reorder level tells the system when
> to warn you that stock is running low. And the sell price is only needed for items
> sold directly to customers — like bottled drinks — not for items used only as
> ingredients. Categories, which you manage on a separate screen, keep this catalogue
> organised for searching and reporting.

### Section 3 — Suppliers
**Estimated length:** ~2:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Suppliers**. Show the list.
2. Create a supplier: contact person, phone, email.
3. Add bank account details and payment terms.
4. Show the supplier's performance history / documents tabs.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll set up suppliers.
>
> Open Suppliers. To add one, enter the company name and a contact person with their
> phone number and email. Then add the supplier's bank account details and payment
> terms. The bank details are essential — without them, the accounting team can't pay
> the supplier or reconcile the payment.
>
> Each supplier record also keeps a performance history and a place for documents, so
> over time you build a picture of how reliable each supplier is.

### Section 4 — Recording purchases
**Estimated length:** ~3:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Inventory → Purchases**. Show past purchases.
2. Create a purchase: select supplier, add items with quantity and cost.
3. Record the batch and expiry date for a perishable item.
4. Save / receive the goods.
5. Mention automatic GL posting and stock increase.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll record a purchase — bringing new stock into the system.
>
> Open Purchases and start a new one. Choose the supplier, then add the items you've
> bought, each with its quantity and cost. For perishable goods, record the batch and
> its expiry date. The system tracks stock by batch using a first-expiry, first-out
> approach, so older batches are used before newer ones, which helps you reduce waste.
>
> When you receive the goods and save, two things happen automatically: your stock
> levels go up by the quantities received, and the purchase posts to the general
> ledger — either as cash paid or as a balance owed to the supplier. You don't have
> to tell accounting; the system does it.

### Section 5 — Stocks and stock takes
**Estimated length:** ~3:00
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Inventory → Stocks**. Show current on-hand quantities and average cost.
2. Go to **Inventory → Stock Takes**. Create a new stock take.
3. Enter counted quantities for items; show variances against system quantity.
4. Submit the stock take.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll check stock levels and carry out a stock take.
>
> Open Stocks to see the current on-hand quantity for every item, along with its
> average cost. SweetTooth values stock using a moving weighted average, so as you buy
> at different prices, the cost updates automatically.
>
> A stock take is a physical count to make sure the system matches reality. Open Stock
> Takes and create a new one. Count each item in the store and enter the actual
> quantity. The system shows you the variance — the difference between what it expected
> and what you counted. When you submit the stock take, it adjusts the records to your
> counted figures and posts any value difference to the ledger. The opening stock take
> at go-live is the most important one, because it sets your starting quantities.

### Section 6 — Material requests and approvals
**Estimated length:** ~2:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Inventory → Material Requests**. Show pending requests from production.
2. Open a request; review the items and quantities.
3. Approve and issue the materials.
4. Show the resulting stock reduction.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll handle material requests from the kitchens.
>
> When production needs ingredients, they send a material request to inventory. Open
> Material Requests to see what's pending. Open a request and review the items and
> quantities being asked for.
>
> If it's in order, approve it and issue the materials. Your stock reduces by what you
> issue, and the materials become available to the production team. This handover is
> tracked end to end, so there's a clear record of what left the store and where it
> went.

### Section 7 — Health checks, expiry, and shift closing
**Estimated length:** ~2:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Inventory → Health Checks**. Show expiry / quality alerts.
2. Act on an expiring batch (e.g. flag or write off).
3. Go to **Inventory → Shift Closing**. Review and submit the inventory side of
   shift close.

**Narration:**
> In this video, we'll cover health checks and closing your shift.
>
> Open Health Checks. This screen flags batches that are expiring soon or have a
> quality concern, so you can act before stock goes to waste. Deal with an expiring
> batch by using it first, or writing it off if it's no longer usable.
>
> At the end of your working day, go to Shift Closing. This is where you finalise the
> inventory side of the shift — confirming movements and closing positions. Submitting
> it closes out your part of the day cleanly, ready for the next shift.

### Section 8 — Inventory reports and sending to the MD
**Estimated length:** ~2:30
**Screen / on-screen actions:**
1. Go to **Inventory → Analytics**.
2. Open Stock Levels, Stock Movement, Valuation, Item Usage reports.
3. Export a report.
4. Compile a report and use **Send to MD**.

**Narration:**
> In this final inventory video, we'll look at reports.
>
> Open Analytics. You have several views: Stock Levels shows what you currently hold;
> Stock Movement shows what's come in and gone out over a period; Valuation shows the
> total value of your inventory; and Item Usage shows which items are consumed most.
> These help you spot slow movers, fast movers, and items to reorder.
>
> Any report can be exported. And when you want the Managing Director to see one,
> compile it and use "Send to Managing Director". That completes the Inventory videos.

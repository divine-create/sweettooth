Getting Started

Introduction

The SweetTooth Accounting Module is the financial backbone of branch operations. This guide covers every page available to the Accounting Manager role.

📂

Chart of Accounts

Master list of all 47 GL accounts. Create, edit, and deactivate accounts.

📅

Accounting Periods

Open, close, and lock accounting periods to control when entries can be posted.

📒

Journal Entries

Create double-entry manual journals. Quick Expense shortcut for single-line costs.

📡

Posting Status

Monitor auto-posted GL entries from sales and purchases. Retry failures instantly.

🔄

Bank Reconciliation

Match GL balances against bank statements to confirm cash positions.

🎯

Budgets

Set budget targets per GL account and period for variance tracking.

📈

Financial Reports

General Ledger, Trial Balance, Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow, Budget vs Actual, AP Aging.

📉

Credit & Debit Notes

Issue credit notes to reduce supplier invoices and debit notes to dispute or add charges.

💳

POS Remittances

Reconcile cash remitted from POS sales against bank deposits.

🏗️

Fixed Assets

Track and depreciate branch fixed assets over their useful life.

🧾

Purchase Payments

Record and settle outstanding accounts payable from purchases.

ℹ️

Role Permissions

Accounting Manager has: view-accounting, manage-accounting, manage-bank-accounts, manage-accounting-periods, reconcile-accounts, view-financial-reports, view-reports, view-analytics, send-reports-to-md.
Getting Started

Login & Clock In

Every session begins at the Staff Login page. Select your branch, enter credentials, choose a shift, and clock in to reach the Accounting Dashboard.

Staff Login — select branch, enter email and password, then click Log In.
1

Open Staff Login

Navigate to /staff-login. Select SweetTooth Port Harcourt (PHC-002) from the branch dropdown.

2

Enter Credentials

Enter your email and password, then click Log In.

3

Select Shift & Clock In

Choose your shift (Morning, Afternoon, Evening, or Fulltime) and click Clock In. You land directly on the Accounting Dashboard.

💡

Direct to Accounting Dashboard

After clocking in, you land directly on the Accounting Dashboard. All accounting features are immediately available from the sidebar — no additional workflow steps required.
Accounting

Dashboard

Your home screen after login. Shows GL posting health, quick actions, and cash & bank account summaries.

Accounting Dashboard — posting health cards (Sales, Purchases, Payments, Adjustments), quick actions, and bank account balances.
📌

Posting Health at a Glance

The dashboard shows pending and failed GL postings for Sales, Purchases, Payments, and Adjustments. Use Review Posting Queue to jump directly to the Posting Status page and address failures.
Accounting

Overview

A consolidated snapshot of the branch's financial position — revenue totals, expenses, outstanding payables, and transaction summaries across accounting periods.

Overview — aggregated financial summary with period-level KPI breakdowns.
Accounting

Chart of Accounts

The master list of every GL account used by the branch. All 47 accounts are organised by type and power every transaction, journal entry, and financial report in the system.

Chart of Accounts — 47 GL accounts with number, name, type (Asset/Liability/Equity/Revenue/COGS/Expense/Tax), and active status.
1

Browse & Search

Use the search box to find by number or name. Filter by type using the dropdown: Asset, Liability, Equity, Revenue, COGS, Expense, Tax.

2

Create a New Account

Click New Account. Fill in the account number, name, type, and optional description. Once saved it is available in all journal entries and reports.

3

Edit or Deactivate

Click any row to edit. Accounts with existing transactions cannot be deleted — deactivate them to prevent future use while preserving history.

⚠️

Numbering Convention

Follow the standard ranges: 1xxx = Assets · 2xxx = Liabilities · 4xxx = Revenue · 5–6xxx = Expenses · 7xxx = Other. Deviating from this breaks financial report groupings.
Accounting

Accounting Periods

Accounting Periods define the financial calendar. You must open a period before entries can be posted to it, and lock it at month-end to prevent changes after books are closed.

Accounting Periods — list of periods with open/closed/locked status and date ranges.
1

View Periods

The list shows all periods with their date range and status: Open, Closed, or Locked.

2

Open a New Period

Click New Period and set the start and end date. Only one period can be open at a time for posting.

3

Close & Lock

At month-end: first Close the period (no new entries allowed) then Lock it (no edits allowed). Locking is permanent — ensure all entries are posted and reconciled first.

⚠️

Lock Only When Ready

Locking a period is irreversible. Always run Trial Balance and complete Bank Reconciliation before locking to ensure the books are balanced and complete.
Accounting

Bank Accounts

Defines the real-world cash and bank accounts linked to the GL. Every payment transaction must reference a bank account registered here.

Bank Accounts — registered accounts with GL mapping and current balances.
1

View Accounts

Table shows account name, bank name, account number, GL mapping, and current balance.

2

Add New Account

Click Add Account. Fill in: name, bank name, account number, and the GL account it maps to (e.g., 1020 - Bank Account - Main).

3

Edit or Deactivate

Use the row action menu to edit details or deactivate unused accounts while preserving transaction history.

⚠️

GL Mapping is Critical

Incorrect GL mapping causes mismatches in the Trial Balance and Bank Reconciliation. Always verify the mapped account code before saving.
Accounting

Journal Entries

The core double-entry bookkeeping interface. Record manual adjustments, corrections, accruals, or any transaction not automatically posted by the system.

Journal Entries — Create Manual Journal form (top-left), Quick Expense panel (top-right), and posted entry list (bottom).
1

Set Entry Details

Choose the Period, set the Entry Date, write a Description, and add an optional Reference (auto-generated if blank).

2

Add Journal Lines

Each line needs an Account, a Debit or Credit amount, and an optional line description. Click Add Line for more rows.

3

Balance the Entry

Total Debits must equal Total Credits. The form shows running totals and blocks saving if unbalanced.

4

Post or Save as Draft

Tick Post immediately to record directly in the GL, or leave unticked to save as Draft for review first.

ℹ️

Entry Status

Draft — saved, not posted  ·  Posted — in GL, cannot be edited  ·  Reversed — voided by a reversal entry
Accounting

Quick Expense

A shortcut for recording a single operational expense. Automatically creates a balanced two-line journal entry — one debit to the expense account, one credit to the payment account.

Quick Expense — fast single-expense form with amount, expense account, payment account, date, and description.
1

Enter Amount

Type the expense amount.

2

Choose Expense Account

Select the GL account describing the cost — e.g., 6310 - Meals & Entertainment, 6320 - Office Supplies.

3

Choose Payment Account

Select how it was paid — typically 1010 - Cash or your bank account.

4

Add Date & Description, Save

Set the date, write a brief description, then click Save Expense.

Accounting

Accounting Entries

A read-only audit view of all accounting entries generated across the system — from sales, purchases, payroll, adjustments, and manual journals — in one unified list.

Accounting Entries — complete list of all GL entries across all sources with date, reference, account, and amounts.
💡

Use for Audit Trails

Accounting Entries is the best place to trace any transaction by its reference number (e.g., SAL-81, PUR-20). Filter by date range or source to isolate entries for investigation.
Accounting

Posting Status

Real-time monitor of all automatic GL postings from sales, purchases, and payroll. Confirm postings succeeded and retry any that failed.

Posting Status — live queue of auto-posted GL entries with success/failure status and one-click retry.
1

Filter by Source

Filter to show only Sales, Purchases, or Inventory postings.

2

Identify Failures

Rows marked Failed need attention. Read the error message to understand the cause — usually a locked period or missing GL account.

3

Retry

Click Retry on any failed row. The system uses an idempotency guard — it will not create duplicates if the original partially posted.

💡

Check Every Morning

Review Posting Status at the start of each day to confirm overnight transactions (closing sales, purchase receipts) posted correctly before generating reports.
Accounting

POS Remittances

Tracks cash remitted from POS sales to the bank. Use this to confirm that daily sales cash has been physically deposited and matches the GL cash entries.

POS Remittances — list of remittance records showing shift, amount remitted, bank deposit reference, and status.
1

Review Remittance Records

Each row corresponds to a shift's cash remittance. Columns show the shift date, sales staff, remitted amount, and whether a bank deposit has been confirmed.

2

Confirm a Deposit

Once you have the bank deposit slip, mark the remittance as confirmed by entering the deposit reference and clicking Confirm.

3

Investigate Discrepancies

If the remitted amount does not match the bank deposit, raise a query with the sales manager. Do not confirm until the amounts reconcile.

Accounting

Expense Imports

Bulk-import expense records from an external spreadsheet or system. Useful for importing payroll deductions, third-party invoices, or expenses recorded outside SweetTooth.

Expense Imports — upload interface with field mapping and import history log.
1

Prepare the File

Download the import template from the page. Fill in: Date, Description, GL Account Code, Amount, and Reference for each expense row.

2

Upload & Map Fields

Upload the CSV/Excel file. The system will auto-map columns — verify the mapping before proceeding.

3

Review & Import

Preview the entries for errors. Click Import to post all rows. Failed rows are shown separately and can be corrected and re-imported.

Accounting

Purchase Payments

Record and settle outstanding accounts payable balances from approved purchases. Links supplier invoices to actual bank payments and updates the GL accordingly.

Purchase Payments — outstanding payables list with supplier, invoice amount, due date, and payment actions.
1

View Outstanding Payables

The table lists all approved purchases with unpaid balances, showing supplier name, purchase reference, amount due, and due date.

2

Record a Payment

Click Pay on a row. Enter the payment date, bank account used, and amount paid (can be partial). Click Save Payment.

3

GL Update

The system posts: Debit Accounts Payable (2010) / Credit Bank Account. The outstanding balance updates automatically.

Accounting

Credit Notes

Issue credit notes to reduce amounts owed to suppliers — for returned goods, billing errors, or agreed discounts after an invoice has been recorded. Each credit note reverses part or all of the original payable and posts the corresponding GL entry.

Credit Notes — list of issued credit notes with supplier, reference, amount, and status.
1

New Credit Note

Click New Credit Note. Select the supplier and the original purchase reference the credit applies to.

2

Enter Details

Enter the credit note number (from supplier), date, and the amount being credited. Add a reason (e.g., goods returned, pricing error).

3

Post

Save and post the credit note. The system posts: Debit Accounts Payable (2010) / Credit Purchase Returns or Inventory account — reducing the outstanding payable.

💡

Apply Against Next Payment

Once posted, the credit note reduces the outstanding balance for that supplier. The reduced balance reflects automatically in Purchase Payments so the next settlement uses the net amount.
Accounting

Debit Notes

Raise debit notes to formally dispute a supplier invoice or request additional charges. A debit note increases the amount the supplier owes you and generates the corresponding GL posting.

Debit Notes — list of raised debit notes with supplier, amount, reason, and posting status.
1

New Debit Note

Click New Debit Note. Select the supplier and the transaction being disputed or adjusted.

2

Enter Amount & Reason

Enter the debit note reference, date, amount, and a clear reason (e.g., short delivery, penalty charge, price adjustment).

3

Post

Save and post. The system posts the reversal entry, increasing the receivable or reducing the payable depending on the debit note type.

Accounting

Tax Payments

Record tax remittances to government authorities — VAT, PAYE, withholding tax, and other statutory obligations. Keeps the Tax Payable GL accounts reconciled.

Tax Payments — log of tax remittances with tax type, period, amount, and payment reference.
1

Select Tax Type

Choose the tax type: VAT (2020 - Sales Tax Payable), PAYE (2120 - Payroll Tax Payable), or other statutory deductions.

2

Enter Payment Details

Fill in the payment date, tax period covered, amount remitted, and bank reference number from the payment receipt.

3

Post the Payment

Click Record Payment. The system posts: Debit Tax Payable / Credit Bank Account — clearing the liability from the balance sheet.

Accounting

Fixed Assets

Track the branch's fixed assets — equipment, furniture, vehicles — and calculate depreciation over their useful life. Keeps the Fixed Assets GL accounts up to date.

Fixed Assets — asset register with acquisition cost, useful life, accumulated depreciation, and net book value.
1

Add an Asset

Click New Asset. Fill in: asset name, acquisition date, cost, GL account (1500 - Fixed Assets), useful life in months, and depreciation method (straight-line or declining balance).

2

Run Depreciation

At month-end click Run Depreciation. The system calculates the monthly charge and posts: Debit Depreciation Expense (6200) / Credit Accumulated Depreciation (1510).

3

Dispose of an Asset

When an asset is sold or scrapped, click Dispose. Enter the disposal date and proceeds (if any). The system clears the asset and accumulated depreciation from the GL.

Accounting

Budgets

Set budget targets for revenue and expense GL accounts by accounting period. Budgets feed directly into the Budget vs Actual report for performance tracking.

Budgets — budget entry grid with GL accounts as rows and periods as columns.
1

Select Period

Choose the accounting period (e.g., May 2026) or budget year from the selector.

2

Enter Budget Amounts

For each GL account row, enter the budgeted amount for the period. Focus on revenue accounts (4xxx) and major expense accounts (5xxx–6xxx).

3

Save Budget

Click Save. The budget is now live and will appear in the Budget vs Actual report against actual postings.

💡

Set Budgets at Period Start

Enter budgets at the beginning of each period before any transactions are posted. This gives the most meaningful Budget vs Actual variance analysis throughout the month.
Accounting

Bank Reconciliation

Match the branch's GL cash balances against external bank statements to identify timing differences and confirm the closing cash position before period lock.

Bank Reconciliation — account selector and reconciliation status overview.
Bank Reconciliation Manage — detailed transaction matching with GL entries vs bank statement lines.
1

Select Account & Period

Choose the bank account and set the statement end date. The system loads all unreconciled GL entries for that account.

2

Enter Statement Balance

Enter the closing balance from your physical bank statement. This is the target balance the system will match against.

3

Match Transactions

Tick each GL entry that appears on the bank statement. The Reconciled Balance updates in real time. Unticked items are outstanding (timing differences).

4

Reconcile

When Reconciled Balance equals the Statement Ending Balance, the Difference shows ₦0.00 and the Reconcile button activates. Click to finalise.

💡

Reconcile Monthly

Complete bank reconciliation at end of each period before locking. This ensures the Balance Sheet cash balance is accurate and audit-ready.
Financial Reports

General Ledger

The complete chronological record of every posted transaction for each GL account. The authoritative audit trail for all financial activity.

General Ledger — transaction history per account with date, reference, debit, credit, and running balance.
1

Select Account & Period

Choose a GL account and date range. The ledger loads all posted entries for that account.

2

Read the Ledger

Columns: Date · Description · Reference · Debit · Credit · Running Balance. Reference numbers link back to source transactions.

3

Export

Download as CSV or PDF for external reporting or audit.

Financial Reports

Trial Balance

Summarises the net debit or credit balance for every GL account at a point in time. Verifies that total debits equal total credits before preparing financial statements.

Trial Balance — all GL accounts with net debit or credit balance for the selected period.
💡

Run Before Month-End Reports

Always verify the Trial Balance first. If Total Debits ≠ Total Credits, find and fix the unbalanced entry before generating Income Statement or Balance Sheet.
Financial Reports

Income Statement

Shows revenue earned and expenses incurred over a period, resulting in net profit or loss. The primary report for assessing branch performance.

Income Statement — Revenue → COGS → Gross Profit → Operating Expenses → Net Profit/(Loss).
Financial Reports

Balance Sheet

The financial position at a specific date — what the branch owns (Assets), owes (Liabilities), and the net equity. Assets must always equal Liabilities + Equity.

Balance Sheet — Assets, Liabilities, and Equity at a point in time.
⚠️

Must Balance

If Total Assets ≠ Total Liabilities + Equity, there is an unbalanced journal entry. Check the Trial Balance and Posting Status to identify the discrepancy.
Financial Reports

Cash Flow Statement

Tracks actual cash movement across three activities: Operating, Investing, and Financing. Explains the change in cash balance between periods.

Cash Flow Statement — Operating, Investing, and Financing activities with net cash movement.
Financial Reports

Budget vs Actual

Compares budgeted amounts against actual GL postings for each account and period. Shows variance (amount and percentage) to identify over- and under-spending at a glance.

Budget vs Actual — GL accounts with Budget, Actual, Variance (₦), and Variance (%) columns.
1

Select Period

Choose the accounting period. The report loads budget figures from the Budgets page and actual figures from posted GL entries.

2

Read Variance

Positive variance on expenses means under-budget (good). Negative variance means over-budget (investigate). For revenue, positive variance means better than budget.

3

Export & Share

Export as PDF and send to management using the Send to MD option.

ℹ️

Requires Budgets to Be Set

This report only shows meaningful data if budgets have been entered in the Budgets page for the selected period. Accounts without budgets show ₦0 as the budget figure.
Financial Reports

AP Aging

Shows all outstanding accounts payable balances grouped by how long they have been overdue — current, 1–30 days, 31–60 days, 61–90 days, and 90+ days. Helps prioritise which supplier payments need immediate attention.

AP Aging — outstanding payables grouped by aging bucket with total outstanding AP summary.
1

Select Date

Choose the aging-as-of date (defaults to today). The report calculates how many days each outstanding payable has been due.

2

Read the Buckets

Review payables by aging bucket: Current (not yet due), 1–30 days, 31–60 days, 61–90 days, and 90+ days (critical — settle immediately).

3

Act on Overdue Items

For anything in the 31+ day buckets, go to Purchase Payments to record settlement and clear the liability. Prolonged overdue payables affect supplier relationships and credit terms.

⚠️

Review Weekly

Run the AP Aging report every week. Payables that age past 60 days can incur late-payment penalties. Keeping the 90+ bucket at zero is a sign of healthy cash management.
Leave Management

Apply for Leave

Submit a leave request. HR reviews and approves or rejects it. Once approved, your leave balance is automatically deducted.

Apply Leave — select leave type, dates, and add supporting notes.
1

Select Leave Type

Choose from: Annual Leave, Sick Leave, Maternity/Paternity, Compassionate, or others configured by HR.

2

Set Dates

Pick start and end dates. The system calculates working days and checks your remaining balance.

3

Add Notes & Submit

Write a brief reason and attach any supporting document (e.g., medical certificate). Click Submit.

Leave Management

My Leaves

Full history of all your leave requests — pending, approved, rejected, and cancelled.

My Leaves — leave request history with status badges, dates, and days taken.
Leave Management

Leave Balance

Shows remaining leave days by type for the current year. Always check this before submitting a new leave request.

Leave Balance — allocated, used, and remaining days per leave type for the selected year.

Monthly Close Checklist

☑️
  1. Check Posting Status — retry all failures
  2. Record all Purchase Payments and Tax Payments
  3. Run Fixed Asset Depreciation
  4. Complete Bank Reconciliation for all accounts
  5. Run Trial Balance — confirm it balances
  6. Generate Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow
  7. Review Budget vs Actual — investigate material variances
  8. Review AP Aging — settle any overdue payables
  9. Lock Accounting Period

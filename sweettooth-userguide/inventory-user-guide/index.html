Getting Started

SweetTooth uses a role-based login system with two entry points: Admin Login for managers and super-admins, and Staff Login for all operational staff. Inventory staff use the Staff Login, select their branch, and are directed to the Inventory Dashboard. Most operational pages require an active shift — your shift supervisor opens this at the start of each working period.

1

Logging In as Inventory Staff

Navigate to the SweetTooth portal and choose Login as Staff.

  1. Open your browser and go to the SweetTooth portal URL provided by your IT team.
  2. Click Login as Staff on the landing page.
  3. Select your branch from the Select Branch dropdown (e.g., SweetTooth Port Harcourt).
  4. Enter your email address and password, then click Log in.
  5. You will be redirected automatically to your Inventory Dashboard.
⚠️
Active Shift Required: All inventory operations (Items, Purchases, Stocks, etc.) are protected by the shift gate. If you see an "Unauthorized" or redirect page, your shift has not been opened. Contact your supervisor.
💡
Forgot Password? Use the Forgot your password? link on the login page. A reset link will be sent to your registered email address.
Fig 1 — Staff login page. Always select your branch before entering your credentials.
📊

Inventory Dashboard

After login, you land on the Inventory Dashboard — a high-level summary of stock health, pending requests, and recent activity for your branch. Use the left sidebar to navigate to any inventory sub-module.

2

Reading the Dashboard

The dashboard gives at-a-glance visibility into the inventory status for the current shift.

  • Summary cards — total items, low-stock alerts, pending material requests, and recent purchase value.
  • Sidebar navigation — all inventory sub-modules are accessible from the left panel, grouped by function.
  • Branch context — all data is scoped to your currently logged-in branch. You cannot view data from other branches.
  • Quick links — common actions like New Purchase or View Requests may appear as dashboard shortcuts.
Fig 2 — Inventory Dashboard showing key metrics and the full navigation sidebar.
📦

Items Catalog

The Items page is the master catalog of every raw material, packaging component, and consumable tracked in inventory. Each item has a SKU, unit of measure (UOM), category, reorder level, maximum stock level, and a current stock value. This is the foundation all other inventory operations are built on.

3

Browsing and Filtering Items

Go to Inventory → Items in the sidebar.

  • Search — filter items by name or SKU code using the search bar.
  • Category filter — narrow the list to a specific item group (e.g., Dairy, Packaging).
  • Status filter — toggle between active and inactive items.
  • Items flagged as Low Stock are shown with a red indicator when quantity falls below the reorder level.
Fig 3 — Items catalog with search, category filter, and low-stock indicators.
4

Creating and Editing Items

Click Add Item (top-right) to register a new inventory item.

  1. Enter the item Name — a SKU is auto-generated but can be overridden.
  2. Select the Category and Unit of Measure (UOM).
  3. Set the Reorder Level (minimum stock before an alert is raised) and Maximum Stock Level (to prevent overstocking).
  4. Optionally enable Requires Request — items with this flag can only be issued via an approved material request.
  5. Click Save to create the item.
ℹ️
Approval Workflow: For non-manager accounts, creating or editing an item submits an audit request. A supervisor must approve the change before it takes effect. Super admin and manager roles save directly.
💡
Bulk Actions: Select multiple items using the checkboxes to perform bulk exports or bulk deactivation in one step.
🗂️

Item Categories

Categories group related inventory items together — for example, Dairy, Packaging, Dry Goods, or Cleaning Supplies. Well-organized categories make filtering, stock reporting, and material requests significantly easier.

5

Managing Item Categories

Go to Inventory → Item Categories to create and manage groupings.

  1. Click Add Category to open the creation modal — enter a name, optional description, and status.
  2. Click any existing category row to edit its name, description, or toggle it active/inactive.
  3. Categories with items assigned cannot be deleted — deactivate them instead to hide from dropdowns.
  4. Use the search bar to find categories by name or description.
Fig 4 — Item Categories — create and manage item groupings for filtering and reporting.
🛒

Purchases

The Purchases page records every stock receipt from suppliers — quantities received, unit prices, batch numbers, and expiry dates. Purchases go through a draft → approval → approved workflow before they affect stock levels. The system also tracks payment status separately from receipt status.

6

Recording a New Purchase

Go to Inventory → Purchases and click New Purchase.

  1. Select the Supplier from the dropdown — contact details auto-populate.
  2. Enter the Purchase Date and optionally an invoice or reference number.
  3. Add line items: for each item select the inventory item, enter the quantity received, unit cost, and UOM. The system auto-fills the base UOM from the item record and handles any UOM conversion.
  4. For perishable goods, enter the Batch Number and Expiry Date per line — this enables batch tracking and expiry alerts.
  5. Click Submit to save as a draft, then request approval. Stock levels only update after a manager approves the purchase.
ℹ️
Batch Tracking: Each purchase line creates a batch record tied to a specific delivery. The system uses FEFO (First Expiry, First Out) logic — the oldest batches are consumed first during dispatches.
💡
Payment Status: After a purchase is approved, update the payment status (Paid / Partial / Pending) as invoices are settled. This feeds the Accounts Payable module.
Fig 5 — Purchases page listing all purchase records with status, supplier, and total value. Use the table/timeline/stats toggle to switch views.
📈

Stock Levels

Stock Levels is the real-time view of the quantity on hand for every inventory item. Each row shows the current available, reserved, and damaged quantities, weighted average cost, total stock value, and health status. This is the primary screen for day-to-day stock monitoring.

7

Reading Stock Quantities

Go to Inventory → Stocks to view live stock quantities.

  • Available — quantity ready for dispatch.
  • Reserved — quantity held against approved-but-not-yet-dispatched requests.
  • Damaged — quantity flagged as unusable (via Health Checks or Callbacks).
  • Average Cost — weighted average unit cost, updated on every approved purchase.
  • Health Status — Good Warning Critical Expired based on expiry proximity and reorder levels.
  • Batch Panel — click any item row to open a side panel showing all individual batches with their expiry dates, quantities, and source purchase reference.
⚠️
Never manually set closing quantities. Stock levels are always auto-calculated from movements. Use a Stock Take or Shift Closing to reconcile discrepancies.
ℹ️
Stock Adjustments: To manually adjust a stock quantity (e.g., after a count correction), click the edit icon on any row. Non-manager accounts must submit an audit reason — the adjustment is held pending supervisor approval.
Fig 6 — Stock Levels showing real-time quantities, health status, and average cost per item.
📋

Stock Takes

A Stock Take is a formal physical count of items on the shelf, compared against the system balance. Any difference (surplus or shortage) is flagged as a variance that must be reviewed and resolved by a supervisor before stock records are adjusted.

8

Conducting a Stock Take

Go to Inventory → Stock Takes and click New Stock Take.

  1. Choose the Stock Take Type: Daily Weekly Monthly Annual Ad Hoc
  2. The system pre-fills the System Quantity column from current stock records.
  3. Enter the Physical Count for each item based on your manual shelf count.
  4. The system automatically calculates and classifies each variance:
    • Match — physical equals system quantity.
    • Surplus — more found than expected.
    • Shortage — less found than expected.
  5. Click Complete to submit. A supervisor must review and approve variances before stock is adjusted.
💡
Best Practice: Run daily stock takes for high-value or fast-moving items, and weekly for everything else. Catching variances early prevents accumulation into large write-offs.
Fig 7 — Stock Takes listing all physical count sessions with type, status, and variance summary.
🩺

Health Checks

Health Checks are quality monitoring records for stock condition. When items are found to be damaged, deteriorating, or near expiry during a physical inspection, a Health Check is logged. The page also surfaces automatic alerts for batches approaching or past their expiry date.

9

Logging a Health Check

Go to Inventory → Health Checks and click New Health Check.

  1. Select the Stock Item affected from the dropdown.
  2. Choose a Condition: Good Fair Poor Damaged Expired
  3. Enter the Quantity Affected — cannot exceed the item's available stock.
  4. Fill in Observations (what you found) and Action Taken (what was done, e.g., quarantined, disposed).
  5. Click Save. Damaged or expired quantities are moved to the damaged stock bucket automatically.
10

Expiry Alerts Panel

The top of the Health Checks page shows two critical alert sections.

  • Expiring Soon — batches with an expiry date within the configured warning window (default: 7 days). Act on these before they become write-offs.
  • Expired Batches in Stock — batches that have already passed their expiry date but are still recorded as available stock. These must be removed immediately.
🚨
Expired stock is a compliance and safety risk. Any batch appearing in the Expired Batches in Stock panel must be quarantined and logged as a Health Check before the end of the shift.
Fig 8 — Health Checks page with expiry alert panels at the top and the health check log below.

Material Requests

Production and sales departments raise Material Requests when they need raw materials or supplies from the inventory store. As an inventory team member you can both raise requests (for internal transfers) and review incoming ones from other departments.

11

Reviewing Incoming Requests

Go to Inventory → Material Requests to see all department requests.

  • Requests move through the following states: Pending → Approved → Dispatched → Completed (or Rejected)
  • Each request shows the requesting Department, the Shift (Morning / Afternoon), the list of items requested, and quantities.
  • Click any request row to open the detail modal — review each item's requested quantity against current stock levels before approving.
12

Creating a New Material Request

Click New Request to raise a request from the inventory team.

  1. Select the Target Department that will receive the items.
  2. Choose the Shift (Morning or Afternoon) the request applies to.
  3. Add line items — for each, select the inventory item and enter the quantity needed. The UOM auto-fills from the item record.
  4. Click Submit to send the request through the approval workflow.
Fig 9 — Material Requests from production and sales departments. Filter by status to focus on pending items.
✅

Material Approvals

Material Approvals is the dispatch workflow. Once a request is approved by a supervisor, inventory staff physically pick the items and confirm dispatch. The system handles all stock deductions, FEFO batch consumption, and routing automatically.

13

Approving and Dispatching Items

Go to Inventory → Material Approvals to process approved requests.

  1. Open an approved request. The approval modal shows each item with the requested quantity and available stock — adjust the Approved Quantity if needed (e.g., partial fulfillment).
  2. The system validates stock availability before allowing approval. If stock is insufficient, you must either reduce the quantity or reject the line.
  3. Click Approve & Dispatch to confirm. The system:
    • Deducts the dispatched quantity from available stock using FEFO — the batch with the nearest expiry date is consumed first.
    • If the requesting department is a Production department, the items are routed to that department's Production Store.
    • For non-production departments (e.g., Sales), items go directly to department fulfillment.
    • A stock movement record and audit entry are created automatically.
ℹ️
GL Integration: Dispatching materials triggers an automatic journal entry in the accounting module, moving the cost from the Inventory asset account to the relevant cost centre.
Fig 10 — Material Approvals — review, adjust quantities, and dispatch to requesting departments.
↩️

Material Returns (Callbacks)

When production or sales departments return previously dispatched items — whether due to excess stock, wrong items sent, damage, or expiry — those returns appear here as Callbacks for the inventory team to review, approve, and reintegrate into stock (or log as damaged).

14

Processing a Material Return

Go to Inventory → Material Returns (Callbacks) in the sidebar.

  • The page shows summary counts: Total Pending Approved Rejected
  • Returns are classified by reason: Excess Return Wrong Item Damaged Expired
  1. Click a return record to open the detail modal — it shows the item, quantity, return reason, and the original dispatch it references.
  2. Click Approve to accept the return. The system:
    • Deducts the quantity from the returning department's Production Store.
    • If condition is good (excess / wrong item): quantity is restored to the main inventory available stock.
    • If condition is damaged or expired: quantity is moved to the damaged stock bucket, not back to available.
  3. Click Reject if the return is invalid — enter a rejection reason that will be visible to the requesting department.
⚠️
Do not approve damaged returns as good stock. Always inspect returned items before approving. Damaged items approved as good will inflate available stock and distort valuations.
Fig 11 — Material Returns showing all callbacks from production and sales, with status filters and summary counts.

Shift Closing

At the end of every shift, the inventory team performs a Shift Close to reconcile all stock movements — purchases received, items dispatched, and returns processed — against the physical closing count. Closing the shift locks the period and sets the opening stock for the next shift.

15

Closing the Inventory Shift

Go to Inventory → Shift Closing at the end of your shift.

  1. The table shows each item's Opening Quantity, all movements during the shift (purchases in, dispatches out, returns), and the Expected Closing balance.
  2. Enter your Physical Count — the actual quantity you counted on the shelf.
  3. Any difference between the expected and physical count is flagged as a variance for supervisor review.
  4. Click Close Shift to lock the period. This action cannot be undone without a supervisor override.
  5. The next shift's opening stock is automatically set from this closing balance.
⚠️
Important: Ensure all purchases and dispatches for the shift are finalized before closing. Any pending transactions after closing must be handled in the next shift and will create an opening variance.
Fig 12 — Shift Closing — reconcile all movements and lock the shift record at end of day.

Suppliers

The Suppliers directory maintains profiles for every vendor your branch purchases from. Each profile includes contact details, payment terms, and a performance history tracking delivery accuracy and price consistency over time.

16

Managing the Supplier Directory

Navigate to Suppliers in the sidebar (under Inventory Management).

  1. The list shows all active suppliers with their contact details and last purchase date.
  2. Click a supplier name to view their full profile, purchase history, and performance metrics.
  3. Open the Performance tab on any supplier to see:
    • On-time delivery rate.
    • Average price variance (how much unit prices fluctuate between orders).
    • Order fulfillment history.
  4. Click Add Supplier to register a new vendor — fill in name, address, phone, email, and payment terms.
  5. Deactivate suppliers who are no longer in use. This preserves their purchase history while removing them from active dropdowns.
Fig 13 — Supplier directory with contact details, last purchase date, and performance overview.

Alerts Dashboard

The Alerts Dashboard is the central view for all critical inventory issues — low stock, overstocked items, expiring batches, and expired batches still in inventory. Check this page at the start of every shift to prioritize actions.

17

Acting on Inventory Alerts

Go to Analytics → Alerts Dashboard from the sidebar.

  • Expired Stock — items past their expiry date still recorded as available. Must be removed immediately.
  • Expiring Soon — batches within the warning window (default 7 days). Plan usage or return to supplier.
  • Low Stock — items below their reorder level. Raise a purchase order promptly.
  • Overstock — items exceeding their maximum stock level. Review procurement frequency.
💡
For each alert, the dashboard links directly to the affected item's stock or batch record so you can act without needing to navigate separately.
Fig 14 — Alerts Dashboard — centralized view of all critical inventory issues requiring action.

Stock Level Analytics

Stock Level Analytics visualizes stock trends over time, helping you identify fast-moving items, seasonal consumption patterns, and items that are consistently over- or under-stocked.

18

Analyzing Stock Trends

Go to Analytics → Stock Levels from the sidebar.

  • Use the Date Range filter to focus on a specific period.
  • Filter by Category to compare items within a group.
  • Charts show opening vs. closing stock over time, highlighting consumption velocity.
  • Items consistently below their reorder line are highlighted in red.
  • Use this data to refine reorder levels and adjust procurement frequency.
Fig 15 — Stock Level Analytics showing trends, reorder alerts, and consumption rates by item.

Stock Movement Analytics

Stock Movement Analytics breaks down every inflow and outflow — purchases, dispatches, adjustments, damage write-offs, and returns — giving a detailed audit trail of how stock moved through the warehouse over any period.

19

Reviewing Stock Movements

Go to Analytics → Stock Movement from the sidebar.

  • Movement Types: Purchase In Dispatch Out Adjustment Damage / Write-off Return In Transfer
  • Filter by Item, Movement Type, Shift, Department, or Date Range.
  • The daily inflow vs. outflow chart helps identify days with unusually high consumption or unexplained losses.
  • Each movement row links to its source document (purchase, material request, or health check).
💡
Use stock movement data during investigations into discrepancies — it provides a full chronological record of every change made to a stock item.
Fig 16 — Stock Movement Analytics — full audit trail of every inflow and outflow by item and movement type.

Stock Valuation

Stock Valuation calculates the total monetary value of current inventory using the weighted average cost method. This figure feeds directly into the Balance Sheet and is used for financial reporting and insurance purposes.

20

Viewing Inventory Value

Go to Analytics → Stock Valuation from the sidebar.

  • Total Inventory Value — sum of (available quantity × weighted average cost) for all active items.
  • Value by Category — breakdown of inventory value per item category.
  • Value by Item — sortable table showing each item's quantity, average cost, and total value.
  • The valuation reflects the current moment — run it at shift close or month-end for periodic reporting.
ℹ️
Accounting Integration: The Inventory Valuation figure here should match the Inventory Asset account balance in the Chart of Accounts. If they diverge, check for unapproved purchases or unposted dispatches.
Fig 17 — Stock Valuation showing total inventory value broken down by category and item.

Purchase Analytics

Purchase Analytics provides a financial breakdown of all procurement activity — spend by supplier, spend by category, purchase frequency, and unit price variance trends over time. Use it to identify cost savings and negotiate better terms with suppliers.

21

Reviewing Procurement Spend

Go to Analytics → Purchases from the sidebar.

  • Top Suppliers by Spend — your highest-cost vendors over the selected period.
  • Category Breakdown — which item categories drive the most procurement cost.
  • Price Trends — tracks unit cost changes over time for key items, helping spot inflation early.
  • Click Export to download the data as Excel for budget reporting.
Fig 18 — Purchase Analytics showing spend distribution by supplier and category with trend charts.

Item Usage Analytics

Item Usage Analytics tracks how each inventory item is consumed across shifts and departments. This is the key data source for recipe costing, waste reduction analysis, and procurement planning.

22

Understanding Item Consumption

Go to Analytics → Item Usage from the sidebar.

  • Filter by Item, Department, or Date Range to narrow results.
  • Total Issued — total quantity dispatched to all departments in the period.
  • Usage by Shift — morning, afternoon, and night breakdown to spot shift-level patterns.
  • Usage by Department — see which production or sales department consumes the most of each item.
  • Items with unusually high consumption compared to their historical average may indicate waste, over-portioning, or recipe deviations.
💡
Share Item Usage data with the Production Manager monthly to align raw material procurement with actual production output and reduce over-ordering.
Fig 19 — Item Usage Analytics showing consumption per item across departments and shifts.

Inventory Reports

Inventory Reports are formal, dated report documents generated by the inventory team and submitted to management through the Reporting Department workflow. Unlike live analytics, these reports capture a snapshot for a specific period and can be reviewed, compiled, and forwarded to the Managing Director.

23

Generating and Viewing Reports

Go to Inventory → Reports from the sidebar.

  1. Filter the report list by Status (Draft Pending Review Approved) or by Type and Date Range.
  2. Click View on any report to open the full detail page — it includes all inventory metrics for the report period.
  3. Completed reports can be printed using the print icon on the detail page.
  4. Reports submitted here feed into the Reporting Department workflow, where they are compiled into branch reports for senior management review.
ℹ️
Report Workflow: After generating a report, it moves to the Reporting Department dashboard where it is reviewed, compiled with other department reports, and sent to the Managing Director for final review.
Fig 20 — Inventory Reports listing generated reports with status, type, and date filters.

Quick Reference — Daily Workflow

Follow this sequence each shift for smooth, compliant inventory operations.

✦

Shift Checklist

Complete these steps in order at the start, during, and end of every shift.

  1. Log in via Staff Login and confirm your shift is active.
  2. Check Alerts Dashboard — act on any expired or critically low stock before operations begin.
  3. Review Stock Levels — verify opening quantities flag any discrepancies from the previous shift.
  4. Process Purchases — record supplier deliveries received and submit for approval.
  5. Review Material Requests — approve or reject incoming requests from production and sales departments.
  6. Dispatch Approved Materials — physically issue items and confirm dispatch in the system.
  7. Log Health Checks — record any damaged, poor condition, or expiring items found during the shift.
  8. Process Returns (Callbacks) — review and approve any materials returned from production or sales.
  9. Close Shift — reconcile all movements, enter physical counts, and lock the shift record.
ℹ️
Key Rules to Remember:
  • Never manually edit closing stock — use Stock Takes or Shift Closing to reconcile.
  • Purchases must be approved before they affect stock levels.
  • Damaged returns must never be approved as good stock.
  • Expired batches must be removed from available stock the same day they are found.
  • Conduct daily stock takes for high-value items; weekly for everything else.
  • Contact your supervisor for any shift-gate or access errors.

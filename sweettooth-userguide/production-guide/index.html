Getting Started

Production staff log in through the Staff Login portal and are automatically directed to their production department dashboard. The system supports multiple production departments (e.g. Hot Kitchen, Pastry, Gelato) and each has its own product catalog, recipe list, and store. An active shift opened by your supervisor is required for most production operations.

1

Logging In as Production Staff

Navigate to the SweetTooth portal and choose Login as Staff to access the production module.

  1. Open your browser and go to the SweetTooth portal URL provided by your IT team.
  2. Click Login as Staff on the landing page.
  3. Select your branch from the Select Branch dropdown (e.g., SweetTooth Port Harcourt).
  4. Enter your email address and password, then click Log in.
  5. You will be redirected to your production department dashboard automatically.
⚠️
Active Shift Required: Most production operations are protected by the shift gate. If you see an "Unauthorized" redirect, your shift has not been opened yet. Contact your supervisor to open the shift.
ℹ️
Multiple Departments: If you manage multiple production departments (e.g., Hot Kitchen and Pastry), each department has its own sidebar section and routes. The department slug is embedded in the URL (e.g., /hot-kitchen/).
Fig 1 — Staff login page. Select your branch first, then enter your credentials.
📊

Production Dashboard

The Production Dashboard is your command center. It gives an at-a-glance view of production activity across all departments you manage. The left sidebar lists all modules available to your role, grouped by department and function. Use it to navigate between recipes, production records, material requests, and reports.

1

Reading the Dashboard

After login, you land on the Production Dashboard showing an overview of your department's activity.

  • The sidebar on the left lists every production page available to your role.
  • Each production department (Hot Kitchen, Pastry, Gelato, etc.) has its own sub-group in the sidebar.
  • Global links — Customer Orders, Reports, Production Helper — appear at the bottom of the sidebar.
💡
Tip: The department slug in the URL (e.g., hot-kitchen) identifies which department's data you are viewing. Switch between departments by clicking the corresponding sidebar group.
Fig 2 — Production Dashboard. Sidebar links are grouped by department and function.
🍰

Products

The Products page lists every item your department produces — both Finished Goods (FG) ready for sale and Work-in-Progress (WIP) items used as sub-ingredients in other recipes. Each product has a type, a unit of measure, and an optional recipe. Only Finished Goods appear on POS screens.

1

Viewing the Product Catalog

Navigate to Products under your department in the sidebar to see all items your kitchen produces.

  • Each row shows the product name, FG or WIP type badge, unit of measure, and assigned recipe.
  • Products marked FG (Finished Good) are sellable items that appear in the POS.
  • Products marked WIP (Work In Progress) are intermediate items consumed in other recipes.
  • Use the search bar to filter by product name.
ℹ️
Adding Products: New products must be created by a manager or admin from the organization settings. Contact your manager if you need a new product added to the catalog.
Fig 3 — Products page showing FG and WIP items for the Hot Kitchen department.
🏷️

Product Types

Product Types define the classification of each product — primarily distinguishing between Finished Goods (FG) and Work-In-Progress (WIP). This classification controls where products appear in the system: FG items show on POS and dispatch screens, while WIP items are internal production intermediates.

1

Understanding Product Type Classification

Navigate to Product Types under your department to view all classifications in use.

  • Finished Good (FG) — A sellable, deliverable product. Produced in full batches and dispatched to the sales department.
  • Work In Progress (WIP) — An intermediate product consumed by other recipes. For example, a bread dough that becomes a finished loaf.
  • Product types are set when a product is created and cannot be changed casually — they affect accounting and POS visibility.
⚠️
Important: Changing a product's type from WIP to FG (or vice versa) affects its visibility in POS screens and stock reporting. Only managers should modify product types.
Fig 4 — Product Types page showing FG and WIP classifications for the department.

Recipes

Recipes are the backbone of the Production module. Each recipe defines which raw materials (or WIP sub-products) are needed, in what quantities, and at what ratio per batch. The system uses recipes to calculate ingredient consumption and generate material requests automatically. WIP recipes are recursively expanded down to base inventory items.

1

Viewing the Recipe List

Navigate to Recipes under your department to see all defined recipes.

  • Each recipe row shows the product it produces, the number of ingredients, and the default batch size.
  • Click a recipe name to view its full ingredient breakdown.
  • Recipes attached to WIP products will expand their sub-ingredients when costing a production batch.
Fig 5 — Recipes list showing all production recipes for the department.
2

Viewing Recipe Details & Ingredients

Click any recipe to see its full ingredient list, quantities, and unit of measure.

  • The detail page shows each ingredient, its quantity per batch, and the unit of measure (UOM).
  • If an ingredient is a WIP item, the system automatically expands it to its base raw materials when calculating batch consumption.
  • The Add Recipe link in the sidebar lets managers create new recipes from scratch.
💡
WIP Expansion: When you produce 10 units of a cake that contains "bread dough" (WIP) as an ingredient, the system traces the dough recipe and calculates the flour, water, and yeast needed — all the way down to the base inventory items. You don't need to manually compute this.
Fig 6 — Recipe detail showing ingredients, quantities per batch, and WIP sub-recipe expansion.

Product Assignments

Product Assignments is a management tool with two tabs: Sales Department Assignment and Recipe Assignment. The Sales tab links products to a specific sales department, making them visible and sellable in that department's POS. The Recipe tab connects a recipe to a product so the system knows what ingredients to deduct when that product is produced.

1

Assigning Products to a Sales Department

The Sales tab lets managers bulk-assign products to a sales department — this controls which products appear in the POS for that department.

  1. Navigate to Product Assignments under your department in the sidebar.
  2. Stay on the Sales tab (the default).
  3. Select one or more products from the product list using the checkboxes.
  4. Choose the target Sales Department from the dropdown (only valid sales departments for this branch appear).
  5. Click Apply Assignment. The system updates each selected product's sales_department_id.
ℹ️
Why this matters: A product's sales_department_id determines which sales department can sell it. If a finished good is not appearing in the POS, the most common cause is that it has not been assigned to the correct sales department here.
⚠️
Approval Required: Non-super-admin users must provide an audit reason before the assignment takes effect. The request goes into an approval queue and is executed once approved by a super admin.
Fig 7 — Product Assignments page: Sales tab (left) for linking products to a sales department; Recipe tab for assigning recipes to products.
2

Assigning a Recipe to a Product

The Recipe tab links a pre-created recipe to a product, so the system knows its ingredients when production is recorded.

  1. Click the Recipe tab on the Product Assignments page.
  2. Select the Product you want to link a recipe to.
  3. Select the Recipe from the dropdown (only recipes belonging to this department appear).
  4. Click Apply Assignment. The recipe is now linked to the product.
💡
Recipe required for production: If a product has no recipe assigned, the system cannot calculate ingredient deductions when you use Quick Produce. Always ensure new products have a recipe assigned before your team begins producing them.
📁

Production Records

Production Records are the official log of every completed production batch in your department. Each record captures the product produced, quantity, date, ingredients consumed, and the staff member responsible. These records feed into cost accounting and ingredient utilization reports.

1

Viewing Production Records

Navigate to Production Records under your department to see the history of all completed batches.

  • Records are listed chronologically with the most recent at the top.
  • Each row shows: product name, batch quantity, date produced, production method, and status.
  • Click any record to expand its ingredient consumption detail — useful for cost audits.
  • Filter by date range or product name using the filter controls at the top of the table.
ℹ️
Record Sources: Production records are created automatically when you submit a Quick Produce entry or complete a production batch request. You cannot manually create a record without going through one of those workflows.
Fig 8 — Production Records showing completed batch history for the department.
⚡

Quick Produce — Finished Goods

Quick Produce is a streamlined form for recording production without a formal batch request. The Finished Goods (FG) variant is used for sellable items that will be dispatched to the sales department. The system automatically deducts raw materials from the Production Store and creates a production record.

1

Recording a Finished Good Production

Navigate to Produce Finished Goods in the sidebar to record an FG batch produced this shift.

  1. Select the Product you produced from the dropdown (only FG products assigned to your department appear).
  2. Enter the Quantity Produced.
  3. Confirm the Batch Date (defaults to today).
  4. Add any Notes if needed (e.g., batch number, supervisor name).
  5. Click Submit to record the production.
💡
Automatic Ingredient Deduction: When you submit a Quick Produce entry, the system automatically calculates the raw materials consumed (using the product's recipe) and deducts them from your Production Store stock.
⚠️
Insufficient Stock: If your Production Store does not have enough raw materials to cover the batch, the system will warn you. Raise a Material Request to replenish stock before producing.
Fig 9 — Quick Produce (Finished Goods) form for recording an FG production batch.
🔧

Quick Produce — WIP (Work In Progress)

The WIP Quick Produce form is used for recording intermediate or semi-finished items — for example, producing bread dough that will be used to bake multiple finished products. WIP items are stored in the Production Store and consumed in subsequent FG production runs.

1

Recording a WIP Production

Navigate to Produce WIP in the sidebar when you need to record the production of an intermediate item.

  1. Select the WIP Product from the dropdown (only WIP items assigned to your department appear).
  2. Enter the Quantity Produced in the product's base unit.
  3. Confirm the Batch Date.
  4. Click Submit. The system deducts the WIP recipe's raw materials from your Production Store and adds the produced WIP quantity to stock.
ℹ️
WIP in the Production Store: After submitting a WIP batch, the produced quantity appears in your Production Store as available stock, ready to be consumed when you produce the corresponding Finished Goods.
Fig 10 — Quick Produce (WIP) form for recording intermediate production batches.

Production Store

The Production Store is your department's raw material warehouse. It tracks every ingredient you have received from the Inventory department, how much has been consumed in production, and the current balance available. All material requests and production batches flow through this store — it is the single source of truth for your department's ingredient stock.

1

Checking Current Store Stock

Navigate to Production Store in the sidebar to view all items currently in your department's store.

  • Each row shows: ingredient name, unit of measure, quantity received, quantity consumed, and current balance.
  • Items with a low or zero balance are highlighted — raise a Material Request to restock them.
  • The store is updated automatically when:
    • Inventory dispatches materials to your department (balance increases).
    • You submit a Quick Produce or Production Batch (balance decreases by the recipe's ingredient amounts).
    • You return excess materials via Material Returns (balance decreases, inventory balance increases).
💡
Daily Check: Review your Production Store at the start of every shift to confirm you have enough raw materials for the day's production plan. Raise material requests early to avoid delays.
Fig 11 — Production Store showing current raw material balances for the department.
📤

Material Requests

When your Production Store is running low on raw materials, you raise a Material Request to ask the Inventory department to dispatch stock to your department. The inventory team reviews, approves, and dispatches the requested items, which then appear in your Production Store.

1

Raising a Material Request

Navigate to Material Request in the sidebar and fill in the request form.

  1. Click Material Request in the sidebar.
  2. Click New Request or the + button to start a new request.
  3. Add each ingredient you need: select the item, enter the quantity, and confirm the unit of measure.
  4. Add a note if urgency or special instructions apply.
  5. Click Submit. Your request enters the inventory approval queue.
ℹ️
Approval Workflow: Material requests go through an approval process in the Inventory module. Once approved and dispatched, the items will automatically appear in your Production Store balance.
⚠️
Plan Ahead: Material requests are not instant. Allow time for inventory review and dispatch. Raise requests at the end of your shift for the following day's needs.
Fig 12 — Material Request form used to request raw materials from the Inventory department.
↩️

Material Returns

If your department received more raw materials than needed — or if an ingredient was damaged or expired — you use Material Returns to send the excess back to Inventory. The returned quantity is deducted from your Production Store and added back to the Inventory stock balance.

1

Returning Excess or Damaged Materials

Navigate to Return Item in the sidebar to create a new return, or Return History to view past returns.

  1. Click Return Item in the sidebar.
  2. Select the Item you are returning from your Production Store.
  3. Enter the Quantity to return and the Reason (excess, damaged, expired).
  4. Click Submit. The return is logged and your Production Store balance is reduced immediately.
💡
Use Return History in the sidebar to view all past returns for your department, including which items were returned, quantities, reasons, and dates.
Fig 13 — Material Returns history listing all past returns with item, quantity, and reason.

Raw Material Tracking

Raw Material Tracking gives you a detailed movement log for every raw material that has passed through your Production Store. Unlike the store page (which shows current balances), this view shows the full inbound and outbound history — when materials arrived, how much was consumed in each production batch, and what remains. It is the audit trail for ingredient usage.

1

Tracking Ingredient Movements

Navigate to Raw Material Tracking in the sidebar to view the full movement history for your department's materials.

  • Filter by date range, ingredient name, or movement type (received, consumed, returned).
  • Each row shows: date, ingredient, movement type, quantity, and the production record or material request that triggered it.
  • Use this view to investigate discrepancies between expected and actual ingredient usage.
💡
Audit Tip: If your Production Store balance seems wrong, use Raw Material Tracking to trace every movement and identify the source of the discrepancy — whether it's an over-counted batch, a missing return, or a failed dispatch.
Fig 14 — Raw Material Tracking page showing full inbound/outbound movement history.

Sales Requests Review

When the sales team needs products that are not currently in stock, they raise a Sales Request asking the production department to produce them. This page shows all pending sales requests assigned to your department. You review each request, confirm whether you can fulfil it, and dispatch produced items back to the sales team when ready.

1

Reviewing and Fulfilling Sales Requests

Navigate to Sales Requests Review under your department in the sidebar.

  • Each request shows the product needed, quantity requested, and the requesting sales department.
  • Review pending requests tagged Pending.
  • Once you have produced the requested quantity, click Dispatch to send the items to the sales team's stock.
  • Requests that have been fulfilled show a Dispatched status.
ℹ️
Dispatch Flow: When you dispatch a fulfilled sales request, the produced quantity is added to the corresponding sales department's product_stocks.addition_quantity, making it available in the POS immediately.
⚠️
Do not dispatch a request until the items are physically produced and ready. Once dispatched, the sales team sees the stock as available in POS.
Fig 15 — Sales Requests Review showing pending demand from the sales department.
🧾

Customer Orders

Customer Orders feed directly into the production pipeline. When a customer places an order for a bespoke item or a large quantity, it is captured here and generates a production requirement for your department. This ensures the kitchen produces exactly what is needed to fulfil customer commitments on time.

1

Viewing Customer Order Production Requirements

Navigate to Customer Orders at the bottom of the sidebar (this is a global page, not per-department).

  • Each order shows the customer name, product required, quantity, and the delivery deadline.
  • Orders are prioritised by due date — address the earliest deadlines first.
  • When you produce the required items, mark the order as fulfilled via the action button.
  • Fulfilled orders generate production records and update inventory accordingly.
💡
Production Planning: Review Customer Orders at the start of each shift alongside your Sales Requests to plan the day's production schedule. Prioritise customer orders with the nearest deadlines.
Fig 16 — Customer Orders page showing pending orders that require production fulfilment.
🤝

Production Helper

The Production Helper is a planning and lookup tool that helps your team quickly calculate ingredient needs for any given production quantity, check recipe scaling, and verify raw material availability before starting a batch. Use it as a quick-check before committing to a production run.

1

Using the Production Helper

Navigate to Production Helper at the bottom of the sidebar.

  • Select a product and enter the batch quantity you plan to produce.
  • The helper displays the full ingredient list scaled to your batch size, pulled from the product's recipe.
  • WIP ingredients are recursively expanded so you can see base raw material requirements at a glance.
  • Compare the calculated ingredient needs against your current Production Store balance to confirm feasibility.
💡
Pre-production Check: Always use the Production Helper before a large batch to ensure you have sufficient raw materials. This prevents mid-batch shortfalls that stall production.
Fig 17 — Production Helper showing scaled ingredient requirements for a planned batch.
📈

Operations Report

The Operations Report provides a summary of all production activity across departments for a selected date range. It shows total batches produced, units output, ingredient consumption totals, and a breakdown by product. Use it for daily shift summaries and weekly management reviews.

1

Generating an Operations Report

Navigate to Operations Reports at the bottom of the sidebar under the Reports section.

  1. Select the Date Range (start and end date) for the report period.
  2. Optionally filter by department or product.
  3. Click Generate to load the report.
  4. The table shows: product, department, total batches, total units produced, and ingredients consumed.
  5. Use the Export button to download the report as a CSV or PDF.
ℹ️
Management Use: Operations Reports are typically reviewed by production managers at end-of-shift or end-of-week. Staff with the correct role can view the report, but only managers can export or archive it.
Fig 18 — Operations Report showing production summary across departments and date range.

Performance Report

The Performance Report analyses production efficiency — comparing planned vs actual output, ingredient utilization rates, waste percentages, and production cost per unit. It helps managers identify bottlenecks, high-waste products, and departments that are over- or under-performing against targets.

1

Analysing Production Performance

Navigate to Performance Reports under the Reports section in the sidebar.

  • Select a date range and optionally a department or product to focus the analysis.
  • Key metrics displayed:
    • Output Efficiency: actual units produced vs expected based on materials received.
    • Ingredient Utilization: percentage of received materials actually consumed in production.
    • Waste Rate: proportion of materials returned or written off.
    • Cost per Unit: total ingredient cost divided by units produced.
  • Charts visualise trends over the selected period.
💡
Improve Efficiency: A high waste rate on a specific product signals a recipe calibration issue or handling problem. Use the Performance Report to identify the product, then review its recipe and raw material handling procedures.
Fig 19 — Performance Report showing efficiency metrics, utilization rates, and cost per unit.

Reports Library

The Reports Library is a central hub for all production reports — both pre-built system reports and any custom reports your manager has configured. Instead of navigating to each report individually, you can browse and launch any available report from this single page.

1

Browsing and Launching Reports

Navigate to Reports Library at the bottom of the sidebar under the Reports section.

  • Reports are listed by category (Operations, Performance, Ingredient, etc.).
  • Click any report card to open it directly with its configuration panel.
  • Use the search bar to quickly find a specific report by name.
  • Frequently used reports can be bookmarked by clicking the star icon on each card.
ℹ️
New reports added by your manager appear here automatically. If you need a report that does not exist yet, contact your manager to have it configured and published to the library.
Fig 20 — Reports Library listing all available production reports in one place.

Quick Reference

Use this checklist as your daily production workflow guide. Follow these steps each shift to ensure smooth production, accurate records, and complete material management.

1

Daily Production Workflow Checklist

Follow this sequence each shift for consistent, audit-ready production management.

  1. Log in via Staff Login → select branch → enter credentials.
  2. Check Production Store — confirm raw material balances before starting production.
  3. Review Customer Orders — identify any orders due today.
  4. Review Sales Requests — check pending demands from the sales team.
  5. Use Production Helper — verify ingredient availability for planned batches.
  6. Raise Material Requests if any ingredients are low — do this early to allow time for dispatch.
  7. Produce WIP batches first (if your FG recipes require WIP sub-products).
  8. Record Quick Produce — FG for each finished goods batch produced this shift.
  9. Dispatch Sales Requests for any fulfilled sales demands.
  10. Return excess materials at end of shift via Material Returns.
  11. Review Production Records to confirm all batches are logged correctly.

Common Issues & Solutions

Troubleshooting guide for the most common production workflow problems.

Issue Likely Cause Solution
Page shows "Unauthorized" or redirects away Shift not opened yet Contact your supervisor to open the shift
Product missing from Quick Produce dropdown Product not assigned to your department Check Product Assignments; contact manager to assign
Quick Produce fails with stock error Insufficient raw materials in Production Store Raise a Material Request; wait for dispatch before producing
Sales Request stays "Pending" after dispatch Dispatch not confirmed Return to Sales Requests Review and click Dispatch again
Production Store balance looks wrong Missing return or failed dispatch Use Raw Material Tracking to trace all movements
Recipe detail shows no ingredients Recipe not configured for this product Contact manager to configure the recipe via Add Recipe
